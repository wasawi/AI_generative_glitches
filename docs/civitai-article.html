🚲 I broke the model on purpose and it drew me a bicycle anyway

Every image in this post came from the same prompt, the same model, and the same seed:

a photograph of a red bicycle leaning against a white brick wall, soft morning daylight, sharp focus

Seed 123456789. Eight steps. Never changed. Not once, not for a single image.

So why does each one look like a different universe had a go at the same bicycle? 🤔

Because I put a node between the sampler and the model that damages the model's thinking while it draws — and the only thing I changed between images is the seed for that damage.

No LoRA. No img2img. No filters. Nothing was done to the picture afterwards. It came out like that.

What does it do?

It reaches inside the model while it's generating and corrupts its own internal numbers.

Your checkpoint is never touched. Nothing is written to disk, nothing is modified, nothing is permanent. Delete the node and the model is exactly as it was. 🔒

It works with

  • 🎛️ Four damage modes: dropout, amplify, sign_flip, noise
  • 🎨 Six noise flavours: gaussian, uniform, laplace, cauchy, spikes, binary
  • 🖼️ Any mask — glitch only part of the picture
  • 📈 Curves — ramp the damage across the steps, or across the model's 28 blocks
  • 🎲 A randomizer workflow that invents a new recipe every time you hit Queue
  • 🧩 Krea2 models, GGUF or safetensors

The settings behind these images

Here it is, the whole thing, nothing hidden:

mode          noise
strength      5.0
probability   0.25
target        both
blocks        0 → 27
steps         0 → 2
distribution  binary
noise_scale   4
mask          512×512 square (or circle), centred

Model: museByStableYogi_v25 (Krea2 GGUF)
Sampler: er_sde / simple, 8 steps, CFG 1.0, 1664×1248, batches of 4

How does it work? (the simple version)

A diffusion model doesn't paint. It thinks in numbers, and the picture is a side effect.

Krea2 cuts your canvas into 16×16 pixel patches called tokens. Each token gets about 6,144 numbers describing what the model believes about that patch: is it metal, is it shadowed, is it part of a wheel, is it in focus. Those numbers pass through 28 blocks, each one nudging them closer to a real image. Do that eight times and you get a bicycle. 🚲

The node steps into the middle of that and scrambles some of the numbers.

With the settings above: at each of the 28 blocks, it picks a quarter of the 6,144 channels — about 1,500 of them — and adds noise five times louder than the patch's own signal.

And here's the important part: it only touches the picture. Your prompt passes through untouched. The model still reads your request perfectly. It just can't draw it properly anymore.

That's the whole aesthetic. This isn't a model that forgot what a bicycle is. It's a model that knows exactly what a bicycle is and has lost fine motor control. 😅

Why only the first 3 steps?

This is the setting that matters most.

💥 💥 💥 ⬜ ⬜ ⬜ ⬜ ⬜
 0  1  2  3  4  5  6  7

Early steps decide composition: where the shapes go, where the light comes from. Later steps decide detail: brick texture, chrome highlights.

Glitch the late steps → crunchy artifacts sprinkled on a normal photo. Looks like a broken JPEG. Meh.

Glitch the first three steps → you break the decision-making before the model has committed to anything. Then five clean steps do their absolute best to render that wreckage as a beautiful photograph. Soft morning daylight. Sharp focus. Applied to something that was never a bicycle. ✨

The model isn't glitching. The model is trying its hardest. The glitch happened three steps ago and it's too late for everyone.

Why is there a mask?

The mask decides where the damage lands. Outside it, the image generates completely normally.

22 of these use a square mask, 7 use a circle. Both 512×512 and centred, so the middle half of the frame gets damaged and the edges stay clean.

The point isn't subtlety, it's contrast. An image that's glitched everywhere just reads as "broken, scroll on". An image where the outside is a calm, sharp, perfectly lit photo of a white brick wall and the middle has fallen apart reads as something happening to a real place. Your eye needs the clean part to understand what it's losing.

Square and circle feel different, too. The square's straight edges cut across the scene like a screen inside the screen. The circle melts into it, as if the damage is welling up from inside.

What changed between the images? One number 🎲

Everything above is identical in all 29 images. Same prompt. Same sampler seed.

The only thing that moved is glitch_seed. It decides which 1,500 channels get hit and what the noise looks like. I just counted upwards and kept the ones I liked.

Some images share a glitch seed — those came from the same batch of 4. Same damage, same everything. They differ only because each image in a batch starts from different noise, a nice illustration of how much of the result is the glitch and how much is ordinary diffusion luck.

Same seed + same settings = same glitch, always. This is an instrument, not a slot machine. Find something you like, write down the seed, get it back tomorrow. 🎯

Installing it

Clone it into custom_nodes and restart ComfyUI:

git clone https://github.com/wasawi/AI_generative_glitches.git /path/to/ComfyUI/custom_nodes/AI_generative_glitches

The nodes appear under experimental → glitches. You need a Krea2 model (GGUF or safetensors — it checks the architecture, not the file format).

The four workflows

  • 🟢 krea2-glitch-smoke.json — start here. Turn enabled on and off with a fixed seed and watch what one setting does.
  • 🎨 krea2-glitch-shape.json — distributions, blob sizes, masks, curves. These images came from this one.
  • 🎲 krea2-glitch-random.json — every input driven by one master seed. Hit Queue, get a brand new recipe every time.
  • 🎲 krea2-glitch-shape-random.json — the same, plus the shape and mask settings. Unplug any randomizer to fix that value by hand.

Good to know

  • 🔒 It never modifies your checkpoint. The damage only exists in memory, during generation.
  • ⚠️ Very high strengths can overflow float16 models (black images or NaN errors). bfloat16 and float32 have far more headroom.
  • 🚫 torch.compile isn't supported.
  • 😅 In the metadata of these images you'll find spike_density 0.05, which does nothing here — it only applies to the spikes distribution, and these use binary. Leftover from an earlier session.
  • 📈 You'll also spot two Spline Editor nodes sitting unplugged. They ramp the strength across the steps or across the blocks. I left them disconnected to keep this set simple. They're a whole other rabbit hole.
